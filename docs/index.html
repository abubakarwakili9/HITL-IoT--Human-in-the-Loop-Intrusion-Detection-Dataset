Beyond Intrusion Detection: Advancing Trustworthy AI for IoT Cybersecurity

A reproducible research benchmark for intrusion detection, predictive uncertainty, confidence calibration, selective deferral and human–AI oversight.

HITL-IoT helps researchers investigate what happens after an AI system detects a potential cyberattack. By combining controlled IoT network-flow scenarios, standardized evaluation protocols and simulated oversight profiles, the benchmark enables systematic study of model reliability, alert prioritization and risk-aware cybersecurity decisions.

Research motivation

Why HITL-IoT Matters

Moving beyond detection accuracy toward reliable, uncertainty-aware and oversight-aware AI cybersecurity.

Intrusion-detection benchmarks commonly focus on attack classification. HITL-IoT keeps that evaluation and adds two complementary research dimensions: how far a model's confidence can be trusted, and what happens when uncertain decisions are reviewed or deferred under a limited budget.

Traditional IDS evaluation
  • Detection accuracy and classification metrics
  • Benign-versus-attack prediction
  • Model comparisons
Trustworthy AI evaluation
  • Confidence calibration
  • Prediction uncertainty
  • Reliability and selective risk
Oversight-aware evaluation
  • Simulated review allocation
  • Decision deferral
  • Intervention benefits and harms
  • Fixed-budget comparisons

The central research question“When should an AI security system trust its prediction, request additional review, or defer a decision?”

Evaluation method

  1. Train on the chronological training split (70%); tune on validation (15%).
  2. Use only the 39 permitted primary features.
  3. Score the test split (15%, 19,177 flows) once.
  4. The reference model is chosen on validation performance.
All nine rows were re-scored from the released probabilities with the official scorer and match the manuscript to four decimals.

Datasheet and data provenance

Documented in the seven-part format of Datasheets for Datasets (Gebru et al.).

Controlled Benchmark Design and Data Provenance

HITL-IoT was developed using a controlled, reproducible smart-home IoT scenario covering 168 hours and twelve modeled devices. The dataset incorporates defined attack behaviors, known reference labels, configurable traffic characteristics and standardized evaluation partitions. This design enables systematic comparisons of intrusion detection, uncertainty estimation and oversight-aware decision policies under consistent experimental conditions.

Provenance. The network flows and oversight profiles are generated. They are not captures from an operational network and not decisions observed from human analysts.

1Motivation

Most IoT intrusion datasets provide traffic features and labels only, so they cannot test what happens after a prediction: deferral, review routing, calibration and the trade-off between correcting and harming decisions. HITL-IoT provides a controlled, repeatable setting for those questions. Created by Abubakar Wakili, Muhammad Idris and Sara Bakkali. No specific funding was received.

2Composition

127,845 flow records with 60 columns: time, addressing, ports and services, device identity, volume and rate statistics, per-device rolling statistics, labels (is_attack, attack_type), construction variables and oversight-profile fields. 85.0% benign and 15.0% attack across twelve attack families in four threat groups. The oversight subset has 10,227 records: 8,160 high, 1,557 medium and 510 low competence profiles. Addresses are synthetic and the dataset contains no personal data.

3Controlled benchmark design and data provenance

Produced in a controlled simulation of a smart home: 168 hours of traffic from 15 to 21 January 2024 across twelve modeled devices. Each attack family has an explicit port-mimicry rate and similarity to benign traffic, so difficulty is known and adjustable. Labels are exact by construction and the release is byte-reproducible (seed 42). The flows and oversight profiles are generated; they are not operational captures or observed human decisions, and no human participants were involved.

4Preprocessing and labelling

Labels are exact by construction. The oversight subset is the 8% of flows with the highest ambiguity score. Version 1.1 is leakage-audited: fields that could reveal the label are removed from the detection features, and a published feature policy separates the 39 detection features from construction variables.

5Uses

Leakage-safe IDS benchmarking, probability calibration, selective prediction, learning to defer, review routing, correction-versus-harm analysis and teaching. Simulated profiles make oversight policies cheap to compare; studies of real analyst behaviour need human participants. For detection on real traffic, pair HITL-IoT with capture-based datasets such as CICIoT2023 and ToN-IoT.

Free under CC BY 4.0 from GitHub. A versioned Zenodo record with its own DOI accompanies the release once published.
