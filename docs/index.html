Version 1.1, accepted in BenchCouncil Transactions on Benchmarks, Standards and Evaluations

Every intrusion detector makes mistakes. Which of its predictions should a person check?

HITL-IoT is a reproducible smart-home IoT benchmark for the questions that come after detection: how confident the model really is, which predictions to send for review, and when intervening helps or harms.

Download the data View on GitHub Cite

What is in the benchmark

A seeded generator simulates one week of traffic in a smart home with five kinds of device, twelve attack families and fixed train, validation and test splits. Every file can be regenerated byte for byte and checked against published SHA-256 hashes.

On top of detection, a 10,227-flow subset carries oversight-profile metadata: a decision, confidence, latency and rationale from configurable review profiles. That makes deferral, routing and correction-versus-harm experiments repeatable.

Read before use

The flows are generated, not captured from a real network. The oversight records come from simulated review profiles, not human analysts, and their profile labels do not measure real expertise. Use HITL-IoT alongside capture-based datasets such as CICIoT2023 and ToN-IoT, not instead of them.

Load it in Python

import pandas as pd

base = "https://github.com/abubakarwakili9/HITL-IoT-Dataset/raw/main/data/v1.1/dataset/"
df     = pd.read_csv(base + "HITL-IoT_dataset_v1.1.csv", low_memory=False)
splits = pd.read_csv(base + "HITL-IoT_splits_v1.1.csv")

df = df.merge(splits, on=["flow_id", "timestamp"])
train = df[df.temporal_split == "train"]      # 89,492 flows
test  = df[df.temporal_split == "test"]       # 19,177 flows

Reference results

Nine models trained on the 39 flow features, scored once on the chronological test split (70/15/15). Reproduce any row with the benchmark scorer.

ModelMacro-F1MCCAUROC

Bars show Macro-F1 on a 0.90 to 1.00 scale.

False-alarm rates stay below 0.7% for every model, but false-negative rates range from 10.2% to 23.2%. The models rarely cry wolf; they do miss attacks. That asymmetry is what the oversight tasks study.

What the oversight experiments found

  • Missed attacks look confident. Reviewing the least-confident 8% of test flows catches 15.2% of the Random Forest's errors after calibration.
  • Calibration fixes the probabilities, not the misses. Isotonic calibration cuts expected calibration error from 0.0186 to 0.0014.
  • Two kinds of "uncertain" barely overlap. The generator's ambiguity and the model's own uncertainty agree almost at chance (AUROC 0.503, Spearman ρ 0.053).
  • Blind override does harm. Letting review profiles overrule every reachable prediction corrects 7 errors and introduces 293. A confidence gate prevents this.

Datasheet

Documentation in the seven-part format of Datasheets for Datasets (Gebru et al.).

Motivation

Most IoT intrusion datasets provide traffic features and labels only, so they cannot test what happens after a prediction: deferral, review routing, calibration and the trade-off between correcting and harming decisions. HITL-IoT provides a controlled, repeatable setting for those questions, relevant to human-oversight requirements such as Article 14 of the EU AI Act. Created by Abubakar Wakili (Euromed University of Fez), Muhammad Idris (University of Passau) and Sara Bakkali (Euromed University of Fez). No specific funding was received.

Composition

127,845 flow records with 60 columns: time, addressing, ports and services, device identity, volume and rate statistics, per-device rolling statistics, labels (is_attack, attack_type), construction signals and oversight-profile fields. 85.0% benign and 15.0% attack across twelve attack families. The oversight subset has 10,227 records: 8,160 high, 1,557 medium and 510 low competence profiles. Addresses are synthetic; the dataset contains no personal data.

Collection process

Nothing was captured. A deterministic generator (seed 42) simulates 168 hours, 15 to 21 January 2024, for twelve devices, with attack models whose port mimicry and data similarity are configured to overlap with benign traffic. No human participants were involved.

Preprocessing and labelling

Labels are known by construction. The oversight subset is the top 8% of flows by ambiguity score. Version 1.1 removes label shortcuts found in v1.0 (for example a constant destination MAC for attacks) and renames v1.0's simulated "annotator" fields as oversight-profile metadata. A feature policy separates the 39 detection features from construction signals.

Uses

Intended for leakage-safe IDS benchmarking, probability calibration, selective prediction, learning to defer, review-routing and correction-versus-harm analysis, and teaching. Not suitable for claims about real analyst behaviour, for operational deployment decisions, or as a replacement for capture-based datasets. Cross-dataset tests show a substantial domain gap to CICIoT2023 and ToN-IoT.
